01
Navigating to the Risk360 Portal
Risk360 · Dashboard · Factors · SDC Read-Only
›
SCENARIO
Risk360 is a 360-degree risk management platform that provides holistic risk measurement, quantifies high-risk activities, and maps contributing factors to frameworks like MITRE and NIST. In this lab you will log in via the Solutions Demo Centre and explore the Risk360 Dashboard and Factors page.
OBJECTIVES
  • Log in to the SDC and navigate to the Risk360 portal
  • Explore the Organizational Risk Score and Risk Score Trend
  • View Risk Events by Location and Contributing Factors by Entity
  • Analyse attack stages in the Factors tab and explore the Tree View
1.1
View Risk360 Dashboard
›

Familiarize yourself with Organizational Risk Score, Risk Score Trend, Risk Events by Location, and Contributing Factors by Entity.

  1. On the Corp: Client PC (or any browser), log in to the Solution Demo Centre at https://sdc.zslogin.net using your ZIA_ADMIN_USER_ID and Session Password.
  2. Click the Risk360 tile to navigate to the Risk360 portal.
    →
    TIP
    You can connect directly from your local laptop — log in to https://sdc.zslogin.net, go to ZIdentity Dashboard, and use your SDC ADMIN_USER_ID.
  3. Click the Dashboard tab in the left menu.
  4. View the Organization Risk Score dashboard — it shows the Zscaler-computed risk score for your organization and the industry peer average, across 4 categories: External Attack Surface, Compromise, Lateral Propagation, and Data Loss. Severity ranges: Low (0–25), Medium (26–50), High (51–75), Critical (76–100).
  5. In the Risk Score Trend tile, click Key Events to view the top 10 events impacting the risk score. Hover over any event to view the score change and contributing factors.
    i
    NOTE
    The Risk Score Trend graph shows the Zscaler-computed and industry peer average trend for the last 90 days.
  6. In the Risk Events by Location tile, view the top risky locations. Hover over any specific location to view its details.
  7. Navigate to the Factors tab in the left menu. Click each attack stage — External Attack Surface, Compromise, Lateral Propagation, Data Loss — to view the Factor, Category, and Risk Score for the past 30 days.
    i
    NOTE
    The Factors page lists contributing factors affecting your organization's risk score. Risk360 quantifies each by risk weight and maps them to MITRE, NIST, and integrations like CrowdStrike.
  8. Under the Entity column, click entities affected by a factor (e.g. 3rd Parties, Applications, Workforce) to view their details.
  9. Switch to the Tree View and explore the parameters — External Attack Surface, Compromise, Lateral Propagation, and Data Loss — by entities: Workforce, 3rd Parties, Applications, and Assets.
WHY THIS MATTERS

The Risk360 Dashboard gives security teams a unified, quantified view of organizational risk scored against industry peers. Understanding the Factors page helps prioritize remediation by showing which entities and attack stages drive the highest risk weight.

View Reports in Risk360
CISO Board · Attack Surface · Cybersecurity Maturity · SEC Disclosures
›
SCENARIO
Zscaler Risk360 provides detailed reports — CISO board slides, attack surface reports, and cybersecurity maturity assessments — that help organizations assess risk posture and assist with compliance and mitigation. In this lab you will navigate and explore each report type.
OBJECTIVES
  • Navigate to the Reports page in Risk360
  • Review all available report categories
  • Understand the purpose and format of each report type
2.1
View CISO Board Report, Attack Surface Report, Cybersecurity Maturity Assessment, SEC Disclosures
›
  1. Click the Reports tab in the left panel of the Risk360 portal.
  2. From the Reports page, view and download the available risk-related reports.
  3. Click View All at the bottom of any category to see all reports for that type.
i
REPORT TYPES
  • CISO Board Slides: Board-ready PowerPoint slides quantifying how Risk360 measures and reduces risk. Generated weekly.
  • Attack Surface Report: Details your organization's internet-exposed applications and servers and their possible exploitation.
  • Cybersecurity Maturity Assessment: Holistic view of your zero-trust journey, generated by a custom Zscaler LLM.

  • Miscellaneous: Risk management materials including board guidance on managing cyber risk.
WHY THIS MATTERS

Risk360's reporting suite bridges technical risk data and executive communication. CISO Board Slides and SEC Disclosures turn risk scores into business-language deliverables, reducing the time security teams spend preparing board-level materials.

03
Viewing Assets in Risk360
Asset Overview · Authentication Status · Risky Asset Inventory
›
SCENARIO
Asset-level risk in Risk360 aggregates and visualizes the total number of assets, highlights risky ones, and provides drilldowns to understand what's driving risk. In this lab you will explore the Assets page across all its views.
OBJECTIVES
  • Navigate to the Assets page in Risk360
  • Explore the Risk Score (3D graph) and Location (world map) views
  • View the Distribution of Assets by Authentication Status
  • Inspect the Authenticated Assets chart and Risky Asset Inventory
3.1
View Asset Page (Overview, Authentication Status, Authenticated Assets, Risky Asset Inventory)
›
  1. Click the Assets tab in the left panel of the Risk360 portal.
  2. In the Overview section, view your assets in both views:
    • Risk Score: A 3D graph with Risk Score, Asset Count, and Days. Colour codes: No Risk (0–1), Low (>1–25), Medium (>25–50), High (>50–75), Critical (>75–100).
    • Location: World map showing assets by country. Hover to see asset count. Bubble size indicates relative count. The Top Risky Locations section (bottom-left) lists locations with the highest percentage of risky assets.
  3. View the Distribution of Assets by Authentication Status pie chart — shows the percentage split between authenticated and unauthenticated assets. Total count is shown at the center.
  4. At the right, view the Authenticated Assets chart — shows types of assets visible in your organization's traffic with their count (authenticated assets only).
  5. Scroll down to view the Risky Asset Inventory table — top 1,000 risky assets. For each asset: Asset ID, Private IP Address, Username, Asset Type, Authentication Status, Risk Score, Last Seen, Location.
  6. Click any Asset ID to open its detailed page — Asset Risk Score, Asset Risk Score Trend, Asset Details, and Asset Location.
WHY THIS MATTERS

Asset-level risk lets security teams move beyond org-wide scores and prioritize remediation at the individual device level. The authentication status view immediately surfaces unauthenticated (shadow) assets that pose unquantified risk.

04
Analyzing Risk with MITRE ATT&CK and NIST CSF
MITRE ATT&CK · NIST CSF · TTP Mapping · Frameworks
›
SCENARIO
Risk360 maps your Zscaler protections to MITRE ATT&CK and NIST CSF so you can see exactly which attack techniques are covered, which are misconfigured, and what can be changed to strengthen your security posture.
OBJECTIVES
  • Navigate to the Frameworks section in Risk360
  • Explore the MITRE ATT&CK coverage legend and TTP tiles
  • Drill into individual TTPs to view product mapping and Risk360 factor mapping
  • Repeat the exploration using NIST CSF
4.1
Analyzing Risk — MITRE ATT&CK Framework
›
  1. Click Frameworks in the left menu. Navigate to MITRE ATT&CK.
  2. In the Legend section on the left, review:
    • Coverage: Donut chart showing the split between techniques covered by Zscaler and those not. The center shows total percentage coverage.
    • Configurations: Number of correctly configured vs. misconfigured policies.
    • Use the arrow (top-right of the legend) to show or hide it.
  3. View the TTP Tiles on the right. Hover over any tile to see details. Click a tile (e.g. Active Scanning) to open the details drawer:
    • Technique name, ID, and sub-technique coverage state
    • Details: Link to the MITRE website
    • MITRE Description and MITRE Category (Detect / Prevent / Remediate)
    • Zscaler Comment: How Zscaler mitigates this technique
    • TTP to Zscaler Product Mapping: Which Zscaler feature covers this TTP and whether it is licensed
    • TTP to Risk360 Factor Mapping: Maps sub-techniques to Risk360 Factors with their current status
    • Notes: Any notes you have added
4.2
Analyzing Risk with NIST CSF
›
  1. From Frameworks, navigate to NIST CSF.
  2. Review the Legend — Coverage and Configurations sections, same as MITRE.
  3. View the TTP Tiles and hover to see details.
  4. Click any TTP tile to open the drawer:
    • Technique name, its NIST ID, and sub-technique state
    • Details: Link to the NIST CSF PDF
    • NIST Description and Zscaler Comment
    • TTP to Zscaler Product Mapping and licensing status
    • TTP to Risk360 Factor Mapping
  5. Explore Sub-Techniques to view sub-technique status, product mapping, and whether configurations are correctly set.
WHY THIS MATTERS

Mapping Zscaler protections to MITRE ATT&CK and NIST CSF gives executives and auditors a recognised third-party framework to validate coverage — while giving engineers a direct line from misconfigured policies to the specific attack techniques they leave exposed.

05
Understanding Financial Risk in Risk360
Financial Summary · Monte Carlo Simulation · Breach Probability
›
SCENARIO
The Financial Risk page quantifies your organization's potential financial loss exposure based on its current risk score, models how Zscaler reduces that exposure, and shows which factors drive the highest financial impact.
OBJECTIVES
  • Navigate to the Financial Risk page from the Organization Risk Score Dashboard
  • View Financial Summary and inherent vs. residual risk
  • Run the Monte Carlo Simulation for loss modelling
  • Analyse Average Yearly Exposure and Breach Probability
5.1
Exploring the Financial Risk Page
›
  1. On the Organization Risk Score Dashboard, hover over the $ icon to view the Financial Risk, then click View Details to open the full Financial Risk page.
  2. View the Financial Summary — the organization's risk-score-based financial exposure and the exposure without Zscaler. This section also estimates how much exposure can be reduced by implementing the recommended actions from the Top 10 Financial Contributing Factors section.
  3. Click Monte Carlo Simulation (top-right) to simulate numerous random loss observations in terms of Inherent Risk, Residual Risk, and Average Yearly Loss.
  4. View the Average Yearly Exposure bar graph — shows average yearly loss in millions of dollars for each risk parameter.
  5. Scroll down to view the Breach Probability section — models the statistical likelihood of a breach given the current risk posture.
WHY THIS MATTERS

Financial quantification bridges the gap between security teams and the CFO/board. Monte Carlo simulation provides a statistically grounded range of outcomes rather than a single point estimate, making budget conversations more credible.

06
Navigating the Deception Portal
Investigate Dashboard · Events · Orchestrate · SIEM Integration
›
SCENARIO
Zscaler Deception deploys decoys across your network. When attackers interact with them, Deception generates high-fidelity, real-time alerts with full attacker context. In this lab you will explore the Investigate dashboard and Orchestrate menu.
OBJECTIVES
  • Access the Deception portal via SDC and navigate to the Investigate dashboard
  • View the Alerts Area, Filters, Customization Options, and Timeline
  • Explore the Orchestrate menu — Rules, SIEM Integration, API Tokens, Containment, Service Connectors
6.1
Viewing Events on the Investigate Dashboard
›
  1. On the Corp: Client PC (or any browser), log in to the Solution Demo Centre at https://sdc.zslogin.net with your ZIA_ADMIN_USER_ID and Session Password.
  2. Click the Deception tile to navigate to the Deception portal.
  3. Click Investigate to open the event dashboard.
    i
    NOTE
    All events generated by deployed decoys are shown on the Investigate dashboard. When an attacker interacts with a decoy, Deception detects the threat, collects attacker intent data, and generates high-fidelity real-time alerts.
  4. On the dashboard, view and interact with:
    • Alerts Area: Graphical representation of all events and connected components.
    • Filters: Filter events using queries and predefined time ranges. Build complex queries using the purpose-built query language.
    • Dashboard Customization Options: Filter by level of detail, threat level, decoy type, etc.
    • Timeline: Manually filter events by time range.
  5. Click any event to view its details, then select a preferred action from the Actions menu: Containment (Zscaler or third-party), Mark as safe, Block attacker IP, or Delete attacker from dashboard.
6.2
Explore Orchestrate Menu in Deception Dashboard
›
  1. Navigate to Orchestrate > Rules to view rules that automate workflows, notifications, and containment in response to threat detection.
  2. View the rules list — for each rule: Priority, Name, Condition, Tasks, Types, Actions. Note the Pause Orchestration button.
  3. Navigate to Orchestrate > SIEM Integration to view integrated third-party SIEM solutions.
  4. Navigate to Orchestrate > API Tokens to manage API tokens for programmatic access — deploying decoys and managing event logs.
  5. Navigate to Orchestrate > Containment to view third-party security solutions for isolating active attackers with automated or manual containment.
  6. Navigate to Orchestrate > Service Connectors to view connectors that forward events and audit logs from the Deception Admin Portal to your SIEM.
WHY THIS MATTERS

Deception detects attackers who have bypassed perimeter controls — decoys generate near-zero false positives since legitimate users never touch them. The Orchestrate menu shows how automated containment and SIEM integration reduce attacker dwell time from days to minutes.

07
Exploring Zscaler UVM
Data Ingestion · Scoring · Findings · Tickets · Remediation · Dashboards
›
SCENARIO
Zscaler Unified Vulnerability Management (UVM) provides a holistic view of your organization's risk by ingesting data from multiple sources, applying custom scoring, and surfacing contextual findings. In this lab you will work through data ingestion, scoring customization, findings exploration, and remediation workflows including dashboards.
OBJECTIVES
  • Ingest vulnerability data from multiple sources into UVM
  • Customize scoring to reflect your organization's risk context
  • Explore contextual findings, drill into details, and see how custom scoring changes severity
  • Operationalize findings into tickets and remediation workflows
  • Build and interpret Risk and Remediation dashboards
7.1
Ingest Data from Many Different Sources
›
  1. Access the UVM portal using the credentials provided in your Lab Access Instructions.
  2. Navigate to Settings > Data Sources to view the list of integrated connectors (e.g. Qualys, Tenable, CrowdStrike, Microsoft Defender).
  3. Review ingested data — each connector normalizes severity and CVE data across tools into the unified finding store.
  4. Verify that asset records have been populated and findings are linked to their respective assets.
7.2
Customize Scoring to Meet Your Organization's Needs
›
  1. Navigate to Settings > Risk Scoring.
  2. Review the risk-raising factors that can increase a score:
    • Is the user or asset deemed risky?
    • Does the asset have PII?
    • Is it open to the Internet?
    • Is the asset considered a crown jewel?
    • Any other contextual information from your data sources
  3. Choose how much these factors will increase your risk score and save the configuration.
  4. In the mitigating controls section, configure whether the vulnerable asset is protected by an EDR tool, WAF, or network security solution, and set how much these will decrease the risk score.
  5. Observe how the custom scoring transforms raw CVSS scores into contextual business risk scores.
7.3
Explore Findings
›
  1. Click Findings in the left panel menu.
  2. Explore all contextual findings — identify issues causing the most risk and look for common trends.
  3. Type Microsoft in the search bar at the top, then press Enter to narrow the results.
  4. Click on a finding, such as The version of Microsoft Edge installed.
  5. View the comprehensive details: which tool reported the issue, when it was last seen, and which fix is needed.
  6. Scroll down to view the Score Details — how your custom business context transformed the original CVSS severity into a contextual risk score.
7.4
Operationalize Risk Findings
›
  1. From the Findings list, select one or more high-priority findings.
  2. Use the Actions menu to create a remediation ticket, assign it to an owner, and set a due date.
  3. Review the ticket creation workflow — UVM integrates with ITSM tools (ServiceNow, Jira) to push tickets directly into existing workflows.
  4. Observe how findings can be grouped by asset, CVE, or business unit to streamline bulk remediation assignments.
7.5
Understanding Tickets
›
  1. Navigate to the Tickets section in the left menu.
  2. Review open tickets — view Status, Priority, Assignee, Due Date, and linked Findings.
  3. Click into a ticket to view all associated findings, the impacted asset list, and remediation notes.
  4. Observe how ticket status changes propagate back to the Findings view, marking remediated findings as resolved.
7.6
Exploring Remediation Dashboard
›
  1. Navigate to Dashboards > Remediation.
  2. View key metrics: Mean Time to Remediate (MTTR), open vs. closed ticket trend, and findings by severity over time.
  3. Identify teams or asset owners with the most overdue tickets.
  4. Use the date range filter to compare remediation velocity across time periods.
7.7
Understanding Risk Dashboard
›
  1. Navigate to Dashboards > Risk.
  2. Review the overall risk score trend over time, broken down by severity category.
  3. Explore the top risky assets and top risky findings to identify the highest-impact items.
  4. Note how custom scoring from Task 7.2 surfaces assets that raw CVSS scores alone would not have prioritized.
7.8
Build Your Own Dashboard
›
  1. Navigate to Dashboards and click New Dashboard.
  2. Add widgets relevant to your role — for example: open critical findings, remediation SLA compliance, top 10 risky assets by business unit.
  3. Arrange and resize widgets to create a view that matches your operational needs.
  4. Save the dashboard and set it as your default view.
WHY THIS MATTERS

UVM closes the gap between vulnerability discovery and remediation at scale. By adding business context to raw CVSS scores and integrating with ITSM tools, security teams drive measurable risk reduction through accountable, time-bound workflows — rather than triaging spreadsheets.

08
Exploring Zscaler Asset Exposure Management
AEM · Asset Discovery · Findings · Inventory · Policies
›
SCENARIO
Zscaler Asset Exposure Management (AEM) provides visibility into your organization's external attack surface — discovering internet-facing assets, identifying exposures, and enabling continuous policy-driven monitoring of your attack surface.
OBJECTIVES
  • Explore the Assets and Findings views in AEM
  • Navigate the Asset Inventory to identify exposed assets
  • Understand AEM Policies for continuous monitoring
8.1
Understanding Assets and Findings
›
  1. Access the AEM portal using your UVM credentials.
  2. Navigate to Assets to view all discovered internet-facing assets. Click any asset to view: hostname, IP address, open ports, technologies, and risk score.
  3. Navigate to Findings to view all identified exposures (e.g. open RDP, expired certificates, outdated software, misconfigurations).
  4. Filter findings by severity and type. Click any finding to view its full details and remediation guidance.
8.2
Understanding Asset Inventory
›
  1. Navigate to the Asset Inventory view.
  2. Explore the full inventory sorted by risk score. For each asset: type, technologies detected, open ports, findings count.
  3. Use filters to narrow by asset type (web server, mail server, VPN gateway) or geography.
  4. Identify any shadow IT assets not previously known to the security team.
  5. Review the inventory summary to understand the breadth of your external attack surface.
8.3
Understanding Policies
›
  1. Navigate to Policies in the AEM portal.
  2. Review existing policies. Each policy defines conditions that trigger alerts when assets violate a security standard (e.g. open port 3389 detected, certificate expiring within 30 days).
  3. Click any policy to view its conditions, severity, and assets currently in violation.
  4. Review how policies map to the overall asset risk score and surface violations as findings.
  5. Observe how continuous policy monitoring detects new exposures as they appear — rather than relying on point-in-time scans.
WHY THIS MATTERS

You cannot protect what you cannot see. AEM discovers assets you don't know exist — acquired subsidiaries, forgotten staging servers, shadow IT — and continuously monitors them. Policy-driven alerting replaces manual periodic reviews with always-on detection.
